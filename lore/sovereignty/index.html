Sovereignty

Two sovereignties, opposite fates — the national kind the record says is being dissolved, and the personal kind it says must be kept.

Codex entryConcepts

Sovereignty appears in six sessions with a split personality — and the split is the teaching.

National sovereignty, in the record’s geopolitics, is scheduled for demolition. The directive is quoted outright in the July 2023 session: “Directive. Destroy all border. Destroy all sovereignty of nation. Unite humanity under one umbrella” — and the April 2020 Morocco reading warns Salim that “sovereignty of your country will no longer exist.” Whether that unification reads as the dark agenda or as the coming planetary civilization depends, as always in this record, on which timeline the session is describing.

Personal sovereignty runs the other way: it is the thing to keep. The May 2020 session frames refusal itself in the term — those who “maintain their sovereignty” against mandates — and the practice stack (belief, the Prime Directive’s consent rule) presumes an individual whose yes and no are load-bearing. The record’s arithmetic is consistent: borders dissolve upward into unity; persons do not dissolve at all.
